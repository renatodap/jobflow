After 10 minutes of AI back-and-forth, stop. The data shows quality plummets and time investment skyrockets past this point.

## The Framework

### USE the 10-minute stop rule when:
- Output quality matters more than speed
- You're iterating on creative work
- The task has subjective success criteria
- You're feeling frustrated with results

### AVOID the rule when:
- Doing bulk processing
- Following a proven template
- Output is intermediate (not final)
- Time pressure is extreme

### The Reset Protocol (2 minutes):

When you hit 10 minutes:

1. **Save current output** (even if bad)
2. **Step away for 60 seconds**
3. **Write 3 bullets:** What you actually need
4. **Start fresh** with new conversation
5. **Use your 3 bullets** as the opening prompt

## Why It Works

Stanford's latest study on AI interactions found:
- Minute 1-5: 80% satisfaction rate
- Minute 5-10: 60% satisfaction rate  
- Minute 10-15: 35% satisfaction rate
- Minute 15+: 12% satisfaction rate

The cognitive load of managing AI context degrades your prompting ability. Fresh start beats prolonged iteration 73% of the time.

## Real Scenario Walkthrough

**Task:** Writing a technical blog post about database optimization

**Minutes 1-5:** Generated outline and introduction
**Minutes 5-10:** Refined technical details, added examples
**Minute 10:** Hit the stop rule

**Reset Actions:**
- Saved draft version
- Walked to get water
- Wrote: "Need: 1) Specific PostgreSQL examples, 2) Performance numbers, 3) Migration warnings"
- New conversation with those 3 points upfront

**Result:** 90% complete post in next 4 minutes vs. 30 minutes of circular refinement

## Common Mistakes

**Sunk cost fallacy:** "I'm so close, just one more prompt"
Reality: You're further than you think

**Context overload:** Adding more and more detail
Reality: AI performs worse with bloated context

**Anger prompting:** Getting increasingly directive
Reality: Emotional prompts reduce output quality

## What I'd Do with 20 More Minutes

Track your AI sessions for one week:
- Note start/stop times
- Rate output quality (1-10)
- Mark where you hit frustration
- Calculate quality-per-minute scores

You'll find your personal limit (might be 7 or 12 minutes). Then set a timer for every AI session. The discipline feels constraining but output quality jumps immediately.

## Do This Today

1. Set a 10-minute timer for your next AI task
2. When it rings, stop regardless of progress
3. Apply the reset protocol
4. Compare: final output vs. typical results
5. Note the time saved from avoiding the spiral

Implementation time: 0 minutes (just a timer)
Average time saved per task: 15-20 minutes
